SKIA: Solar Kinetic Interception Array

An independent concept study for an orbital system that shades the Earth from L1 and beams clean power from geostationary orbit, both built from one shared module.

The idea

SKIA (Solar Kinetic Interception Array) is two fleets built from one shared part, an octagonal module 960 m wide. In geostationary orbit, power stations beam microwaves to ground receivers. At the Sun–Earth L1 point, lighter shade modules block a small fraction of sunlight. One fleet cuts emissions at the source; the other buys time against warming that is already locked in.

What the study covers

  • The physics: radiative forcing, safe shading limits, and why the shade goes at L1 but the power stations go at GEO.
  • First-order maths for shade area and mass, delivered power, beam spot size and launch count.
  • System architecture, a hardware bill of materials, ground rectenna design and siting at retired power plants.
  • A phased launch plan, costs, risks and the environmental return.

Current verdict

The physics is plausible. The engineering is unproven at scale, and economics and governance are the deciding unknowns. This is a concept study built on published research and first-order calculations. It has not been peer reviewed.
