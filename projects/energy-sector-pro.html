The question

AI and data-centre demand has made power producers and nuclear fuel a market theme. I wanted to know how a three-stock sleeve (Constellation Energy, Vistra, Cameco) actually behaves. How does it compare with the energy-sector benchmark (XLE)? What drives it? And what does its range of outcomes look like, beyond a single bull or bear price target?

Method

  • Data. Monthly prices from Jan-2025 to Sep-2026 for the three stocks, XLE and four macro factors (WTI crude, Henry Hub gas, the US 10-year yield, uranium spot), all from a single CSV.
  • Risk. Returns, volatility, Sharpe and Sortino ratios, running-peak maximum drawdown, and historical VaR/CVaR at 95% and 99%.
  • Benchmark and factors. Beta, Jensen's alpha, tracking error, information ratio and up/down capture against XLE, plus sensitivities to oil, gas, uranium and rates.
  • Optimisation. A 66-point mean-variance frontier that identifies the maximum-Sharpe and minimum-volatility portfolios.
  • Simulation. 10,000 correlated 12-month paths (Cholesky-decomposed covariance) for the recommended 40/35/25 weights.

A Python pipeline (pandas, NumPy, openpyxl) generates the workbook. Changing the data file and re-running two scripts rebuilds every statistic and chart.

Distribution of 12-month returns

The distribution is strongly right-skewed. The mean (+14%) sits well above the median (+6%), and almost half of all paths still lose money. A single bull, base and bear price target would hide that.

Why I did not just follow the optimiser

On this sample the maximum-Sharpe portfolio is 100% Cameco, the name with the best trailing 20 months. Mean-variance optimisation on so few observations tends to pile into recent winners. The recommendation (40% CEG, 35% VST, 25% CCJ) deliberately underweights Cameco because of its earnings volatility and valuation.

Limitations

  • Twenty monthly returns are a thin sample, so every estimate is directional rather than precise.
  • Factor betas are univariate, and the macro factors are correlated with each other.
  • The simulation assumes normal returns, so it does not capture fat tails or regime shifts.
  • The WTI and uranium series are reconstructed approximations and should be replaced with a vendor feed before real use.

This is independent research for learning and discussion. It is not investment advice.
